## Overview

Heavy rainfall events evolve in both space and time, yet their movement characteristics are often overlooked when analysing extreme precipitation. Understanding how these events propagate is important for characterising regional rainfall variability and associated hydrological risks.

This study introduces the concept of **heavy rainfall trajectories (HRTs)**, defined by tracking the movement of rainfall centres during individual heavy rainfall events. By combining trajectory-based diagnostics, Euclidean-distance measures of dispersion, and empirical orthogonal function (EOF) analysis, we investigate the spatial distribution, movement characteristics, and temporal evolution of heavy rainfall trajectories across China.

## Key Findings

**1. Distinct geographical and seasonal patterns**

Heavy rainfall trajectories exhibit pronounced spatial and seasonal variability across China. Their distribution and movement characteristics are strongly influenced by the East Asian summer monsoon.

**2. Regional differences in trajectory dispersion and length**

From southeast to northwest China, the dispersion degree of heavy rainfall trajectories increases to 8, while their lengths decrease by approximately 54 km, highlighting pronounced regional differences in rainfall movement.

**3. Changes in trajectory orientation**

EOF analysis reveals significant trends in trajectory direction angles across China. Northeast-oriented trajectories exhibit a negative trend, while southeast-, southwest-, and northwest-oriented trajectories show positive trends. During 2008–2019, northeast- and southwest-oriented trajectories become flatter, whereas southeast- and northwest-oriented trajectories become steeper.

## Significance

By examining heavy rainfall as a moving phenomenon rather than solely through rainfall intensity or accumulation at fixed locations, this study provides a complementary perspective on extreme precipitation variability.

The trajectory-based framework offers insights into regional rainfall evolution and provides useful information for understanding and assessing heavy rainfall-related hazards.
